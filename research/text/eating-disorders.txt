Eating Disorders

Eating disorders are serious mental health conditions that involve unhealthy and often dangerous relationships with food, body image, and weight. They go far beyond simple dieting or concerns about appearance, as they can deeply affect a person’s physical health, emotional well-being, and daily life. Common eating disorders include anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, and binge-eating disorder, each characterized by different patterns of disordered eating and thought processes.

People struggling with eating disorders often experience intense fear of gaining weight, distorted body image, and feelings of shame or guilt around eating. In anorexia, individuals may severely restrict food intake, leading to extreme weight loss and malnutrition. Bulimia typically involves cycles of binge eating followed by behaviors such as vomiting or excessive exercise to avoid weight gain. Binge-eating disorder is marked by recurrent episodes of eating large amounts of food in a short period, often accompanied by feelings of loss of control and emotional distress.

The causes of eating disorders are complex, involving a mix of biological, psychological, and social factors. Pressure from media and society to look a certain way, low self-esteem, past trauma, or family influences can all contribute. Genetics and brain chemistry also play a role in making some individuals more vulnerable.
