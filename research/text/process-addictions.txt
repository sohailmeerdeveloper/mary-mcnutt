Process Addictions

Process addictions, also known as behavioral addictions, are compulsive behaviors that people engage in repeatedly, even when these behaviors cause harm to their health, relationships, or daily functioning. Unlike substance addictions, which involve dependence on drugs or alcohol, process addictions are linked to activities such as gambling, shopping, eating, internet use, gaming, sex, or even work. At first, these activities may seem harmless or enjoyable, but over time they can take on a compulsive nature, where the person feels an uncontrollable urge to continue, despite the negative consequences.

The core issue with process addictions lies in the brain’s reward system. Engaging in these behaviors releases chemicals such as dopamine, which create feelings of pleasure and satisfaction. Over time, the brain begins to crave these “highs,” leading individuals to repeat the activity more often, in greater intensity, and often at the expense of other responsibilities. Just like with substance addictions, people struggling with process addictions may experience denial, loss of control, preoccupation with the activity, and withdrawal-like symptoms when they try to stop.

The consequences of process addictions can be wide-ranging. Financial problems may arise from compulsive shopping or gambling. Emotional distress and relationship breakdowns are common when someone prioritizes an addictive behavior over their loved ones. Academic or work performance can decline if time and energy are consumed by gaming, internet use, or overworking. Additionally, process addictions often coexist with mental health conditions such as anxiety, depression, or low self-esteem, creating a cycle that becomes difficult to break without intervention.
